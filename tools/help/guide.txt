[generate_options]
AMB DRIFT uses odd loops with a three-step gate. Layers rarely strike together.
EUCLIDEAN spaces hits as evenly as possible over 16 steps. Density adds hits; each layer starts at a different offset.
ODD LOOPS repeats each layer after 5, 7, 11 or 13 steps. Their accents meet in different places each pass.
POLYRHYTHM crosses three-step and seven-step pulses and skips the last sixteenth of each beat.
SPARSE leaves most beats empty. Higher density gives each quarter note a better chance to sound.
TIDAL swells and recedes on eighth notes. Each layer reaches the crest at a different time.
BROKEN repeats a syncopated 23-step figure. Density decides whether its last accent sounds.
SLOW CLOCK gives each layer one cue in two bars, with entrances offset between layers.
GARDEN gives eligible instruments repeating quarter-note loops. Their scale degrees follow the changing harmony.
AMB BREATHE gradually opens and closes the chance of a phrase, offset across layers over 24 bars.
LONG ARC shares one slow swell across the ensemble. Activity rises and falls over 128 bars.
WANDER shifts the silent bar in a seven-bar pattern. A new offset arrives every three bars.
BLOOM brings layers in one by one, then withdraws them. The last bar of each 32-bar cycle rests.
CALL ANSWER trades groups of layers in two-bar blocks, leaving room for the other voices.
DRIFT SHIFT opens a 24-step window in a 37-step cycle. Its start slips every four bars.
MEMORY returns to two familiar four-bar windows in each 16-bar cycle, with occasional rests inside.
MINIMAL allows half-note cues in only the first two bars of each 16-bar cycle.
ACID LINE turns rhythm cues into short accented runs with slides on automatic Bass and Lead.
LOCKED LOOP remembers an eight-note cell and repeats its pitches or drum voices as cues arrive.
DRIFT LOOP repeats a remembered cell and occasionally replaces one member, so the motif changes gradually.
MARKOV WALK learns transitions from the opening cell and chooses its next move from those connections.
BLEND lets each layer answer its own cues. Several parts can enter together.
STAGGER offsets each layer's rhythm and phrase clock by three more steps, splitting their entrances.
ALTERNATE reserves one bar for each layer in turn. Each still needs its own rhythm cue.
COMPLEMENT gives priority to the first sounding layer and can fill empty beats with a single reply.
SPARSE TALK allows one voice at a time and thins each part to a different three-step alignment.
TIDE TOGETHER swells the layers together and rests them for the last four bars of every twelve.
